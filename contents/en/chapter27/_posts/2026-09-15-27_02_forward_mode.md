What if you could compute a function’s value *and* its directional derivative simultaneously, in a single forward sweep, with zero extra memory for storing tapes? That is the elegance of **Forward-Mode Automatic Differentiation** (also known as dual-number arithmetic). By attaching a tiny tangent $$\dot{v}$$ to every scalar $$v$$—formally writing $$v + \dot{v}\epsilon$$ with $$\epsilon^2 = 0$$—every elementary operation automatically propagates exact Jacobian-vector products (JVPs) right alongside the primal computation.

## Background Knowledge Map: Dual Numbers, Tangents, and Jacobian-Vector Products

- **Dual Number Algebra ($$\epsilon^2 = 0$$):** Carrying pairs $$(v, \dot{v})$$ under arithmetic rules $$(a, \dot{a}) + (b, \dot{b}) = (a+b, \dot{a}+\dot{b})$$ and $$(a, \dot{a})(b, \dot{b}) = (ab, \dot{a}b + a\dot{b})$$ computes exact derivatives via pure operator overloading—no graph tape required!
- **One Pass = One Column of the Jacobian (JVP):** Seeding the input tangent $$\dot{x} = u \in \mathbb{R}^n$$ computes the directional derivative $$J_f(x) u \in \mathbb{R}^m$$ in a single forward pass. Setting $$u = e_j$$ extracts the $$j$$-th column of $$J_f(x)$$.
- **Complexity Sweet Spot ($$n \ll m$$):** Computing the full Jacobian $$J_f(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$$ costs $$n$$ forward passes. Forward mode therefore dominates whenever inputs are few and outputs are many (e.g., differentiating a 1000-output ODE trajectory with respect to 3 physical parameters).
- **Constant Memory Footprint:** Unlike reverse mode, forward mode never needs to store intermediate activations for a backward sweep—making it ultra-lightweight in memory-constrained scientific simulations.

### Step-by-Step Worked Example (Read Slowly): Computing a JVP with Dual Numbers

Let $$f(x_1, x_2) = (x_1^2 x_2,\ \ln(x_1) + x_2)^\top$$ at $$x = (1, 2)^\top$$. We want the directional derivative (JVP) along seed direction $$\dot{x} = (3, -1)^\top$$.

1. **Initialize dual numbers:**
   - $$v_1 = (x_1, \dot{x}_1) = (1,\ 3)$$,
   - $$v_2 = (x_2, \dot{x}_2) = (2,\ -1)$$.
2. **Propagate forward through each elementary op:**
   - $$v_3 = v_1^2 = (1^2,\ 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 3) = (1,\ 6)$$.
   - $$y_1 = v_3 \cdot v_2 = (1 \cdot 2,\ 6 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot (-1)) = (2,\ 11)$$.
   - $$v_4 = \ln(v_1) = (\ln 1,\ \frac{1}{1}\cdot 3) = (0,\ 3)$$.
   - $$y_2 = v_4 + v_2 = (0 + 2,\ 3 + (-1)) = (2,\ 2)$$.
3. **Read off both value and JVP in one pass:**
   - Primal output: $$f(1, 2) = (2, 2)^\top$$.
   - Exact JVP: $$J_f(1, 2)\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 11 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$$.
4. **Cross-check with the full Jacobian:** $$J_f(x_1, x_2) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 x_1 x_2 & x_1^2 \\ 1/x_1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \big|_{(1,2)} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$, and $$\begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 11 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$$. Exact!

Associate to every node $$v$$ a **tangent** $$\dot v = \frac{\partial v}{\partial t}$$ along a chosen input direction (one scalar seed). If $$v=h(u,w)$$ then

$$
\dot v = \partial_u h\cdot \dot u + \partial_w h\cdot \dot w.
$$

Running the original program *together with* these tangent recurrences yields a **Jacobian–vector product** $$J_f(x)\, \dot x$$ at essentially a small constant factor times the cost of $$f$$. That is **forward-mode AD**.

If $$f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$$ with $$n\ll m$$ (few inputs, many outputs), forward mode is cheap: $$n$$ seeded sweeps give the full Jacobian. If $$n\gg m$$ — one loss, a million weights — forward mode is the wrong default. You would need a million sweeps.

Finite differences $$[f(x+\varepsilon e_i)-f(x)]/\varepsilon$$ approximate the same Jacobian–vector products but waste precision and need a step $$\varepsilon$$. Forward AD is exact up to floating-point, with no $$\varepsilon$$.

**Tiny walk-through.** $$f(x_1,x_2)=x_1 x_2 + \sin(x_1)$$, seed $$\dot x=(1,0)$$. Then $$\dot f = x_2 + \cos(x_1)$$, which is $$\partial_{x_1}f$$. A second seed $$(0,1)$$ yields $$\partial_{x_2}f=x_1$$.

Forward mode is what you want for sensitivities of a *simulation* with few parameters (ODE shooting, some Hessian–vector recipes via “forward-over-reverse”). Deep learning almost always wants the next lesson.
