Here is the exact algebraic machinery of **ADMM** in its canonical unscaled form. Whenever you face a convex objective that mixes two incompatible structures—say, a quadratic loss plus an $$\ell_1$$ penalty, or a trace norm plus box constraints—you introduce a duplicate variable $$z$$ coupled by $$Ax + Bz = c$$, write down the augmented Lagrangian $$L_\rho(x, z, u)$$, and cycle through three lines: $$x$$-minimization, $$z$$-minimization, and dual ascent with step size $$\rho$$.

## Background Knowledge Map: The Three-Line ADMM Engine

- **Augmented Lagrangian $$L_\rho(x, z, u)$$:** Combines $$f(x) + g(z) + u^T(Ax + Bz - c) + \frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax + Bz - c\|_2^2$$. Every term in the $$x$$-step sees $$z^{(k-1)}$$ as a constant, and every term in the $$z$$-step sees $$x^{(k)}$$ as a constant.
- **Primal Residual ($$r^{(k)} = Ax^{(k)} + Bz^{(k)} - c$$):** Measures violation of the coupling constraint. Dual optimality of the $$z$$-update holds automatically at every iteration because $$u^{(k)} = u^{(k-1)} + \rho r^{(k)}$$ matches the optimality condition of the $$z$$-subproblem!
- **Dual Residual ($$s^{(k)} = \rho A^T B(z^{(k)} - z^{(k-1)})$$):** Measures violation of the *first* block's stationarity condition (since $$x^{(k)}$$ was minimized against the *old* $$z^{(k-1)}$$ rather than $$z^{(k)}$$). When both $$\|r^{(k)}\|_2 \to 0$$ and $$\|s^{(k)}\|_2 \to 0$$, all KKT conditions are satisfied!
- **Role of the Penalty Parameter $$\rho > 0$$:** While ADMM converges for *any* fixed $$\rho > 0$$, balancing $$\rho$$ so $$\|r^{(k)}\|$$ and $$\|s^{(k)}\|$$ stay on the same order of magnitude dramatically accelerates practical speed.

### Step-by-Step Worked Example (Read Slowly): Computing Primal & Dual Residuals in Unscaled ADMM

Solve $$\min_{x, z} \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)^2 + \frac{1}{2}z^2$$ subject to $$x - z = 0$$ (so $$A = 1, B = -1, c = 0$$; exact optimum $$x^\star = z^\star = 2, u^\star = 2$$) with $$\rho = 1$$, starting at $$z^0 = 0, u^0 = 0$$.

1. **Iteration 1:**
   - **$$x$$-step:** $$\min_x \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)^2 + 0(x - 0) + \frac{1}{2}(x - 0)^2 \implies (x - 4) + x = 0 \implies x^1 = 2.0$$.
   - **$$z$$-step:** $$\min_z \frac{1}{2}z^2 + 0(2 - z) + \frac{1}{2}(2 - z)^2 \implies z - (2 - z) = 0 \implies z^1 = 1.0$$.
   - **$$u$$-step:** $$u^1 = u^0 + 1(x^1 - z^1) = 0 + (2 - 1) = 1.0$$.
   - **Check residuals:** Primal $$r^1 = x^1 - z^1 = 1.0$$; Dual $$s^1 = \rho A^T B (z^1 - z^0) = 1(1)(-1)(1 - 0) = -1.0$$.
2. **Iteration 2:**
   - **$$x$$-step:** $$(x - 4) + 1 + (x - 1) = 0 \implies 2x = 4 \implies x^2 = 2.0$$.
   - **$$z$$-step:** $$z - 1 - (2 - z) = 0 \implies 2z = 3 \implies z^2 = 1.5$$.
   - **$$u$$-step:** $$u^2 = 1.0 + (2.0 - 1.5) = 1.5$$.
   - **Check residuals:** Primal $$r^2 = 0.5$$; Dual $$s^2 = -1(1.5 - 1.0) = -0.5$$.
   Both primal and dual residuals halved cleanly in one iteration, converging geometrically at rate $$1/2$$ directly to $$(x^\star, z^\star, u^\star) = (2, 2, 2)$$!

# Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)

The **Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)** is a powerful optimization algorithm that combines the benefits of dual decomposition and the method of multipliers. It is particularly effective for solving convex optimization problems that can be decomposed into smaller, more manageable subproblems.

Consider the following problem:
>\begin{equation}
\min_{x,z} f(x) + g(z) \quad \text{subject to} \quad Ax + Bz = c
\end{equation}

As before, we can augment the objective function as follows:
>\begin{equation}
\min_{x,z} f(x) + g(z) + \frac{\rho}{2} \lVert Ax + Bz - c \rVert_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad Ax + Bz = c
\end{equation}

where $$\rho > 0$$ is the penalty parameter.

## Augmented Lagrangian

The augmented Lagrangian can be defined as:
>\begin{equation}
L_{\rho} (x,z,u) = f(x) + g(z) + u^T(Ax + Bz - c) + \frac{\rho}{2} \lVert Ax + Bz - c \rVert_2^2
\end{equation}

## ADMM Algorithm

ADMM performs the following iterative steps for $$k=1,2,3,\ldots$$:

> $$
> \begin{alignat}{1}
> x^{(k)} & = \arg\min_x  L_{\rho} (x,z^{(k-1)},u^{(k-1)}) \\
> z^{(k)} & = \arg\min_z  L_{\rho} (x^{(k)},z,u^{(k-1)}) \\
> u^{(k)} & = u^{(k-1)} + \rho (Ax^{(k)} + Bz^{(k)} - c) 
> \end{alignat}
> $$

**Key Point:** It is crucial that $$x^{(k)}$$ obtained from the first step is used in the second step to compute $$z^{(k)}$$. This alternating update structure is essential for convergence.

## Comparison with Method of Multipliers

Note that in the general **Method of Multipliers**, the first two steps are replaced by the following joint minimization:
>\begin{equation}
(x^{(k)}, z^{(k)}) = \arg\min_{x,z} L_{\rho} (x,z,u^{(k-1)})     
\end{equation}

The key advantage of ADMM is that it decomposes this joint minimization into two separate, simpler subproblems that can often be solved more efficiently or even in closed form.
