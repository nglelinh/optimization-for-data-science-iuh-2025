At the end of Section 20-03 we hit a classic engineering dilemma: **dual decomposition** splits big problems into parallel pieces but diverges without strong convexity; the **augmented Lagrangian method** converges robustly even for nonsmooth linear problems but re-couples the variables through $$\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax + Bz - c\|_2^2$$. The **Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)** breaks that stalemate with a deceptively simple Gauss–Seidel trick: instead of minimizing the augmented Lagrangian over $$(x, z)$$ *jointly*, minimize over $$x$$ first (holding $$z$$ fixed), then over $$z$$ (holding the fresh $$x$$ fixed), then update the dual price $$u$$!

## Key ideas: Bridging Decomposition and Augmentation

- **Two-Block Splitting Template:** ADMM targets $$\min_{x,z} f(x) + g(z)$$ subject to $$Ax + Bz = c$$, separating a problem into two simpler pieces (for example, smooth data fidelity $$f(x)$$ and nonsmooth sparsity $$g(z)$$).
- **Gauss–Seidel Alternating Minimization:** Updating $$x^{(k)}$$ with $$z^{(k-1)}$$ frozen and then $$z^{(k)}$$ with $$x^{(k)}$$ frozen keeps each subproblem small and separable—often reducing both updates to closed-form linear solves or proximal operators.
- **Preserving Robustness Without Strong Convexity:** Remarkably, taking just *one* alternating pass over $$x$$ and $$z$$ before updating $$u$$ is enough to retain global convergence for general closed convex $$f$$ and $$g$$ for *any* $$\rho > 0$$!
- **Why ADMM Dominates Modern Distributed Optimization:** From Lasso and Robust PCA to consensus federated learning, ADMM turns intractable coupled convex programs into modular pipelines of tiny subproblems.

### Worked example: One Alternating Pass vs. Joint Minimization

Consider splitting $$\min_{x,z} \frac{1}{2}x^2 + |z|$$ subject to $$x - z = 0$$ (exact optimum $$x^\star = z^\star = 0$$) with $$\rho = 1$$, starting from $$z^0 = 2, \; u^0 = 0$$.

1. **$$x$$-update (smooth quadratic only, holding $$z^0 = 2$$ fixed):**
   $$x^1 = \arg\min_x \; \frac{1}{2}x^2 + 0(x - 2) + \frac{1}{2}(x - 2)^2 \implies x + (x - 2) = 0 \implies x^1 = 1.$$
2. **$$z$$-update (nonsmooth $$\ell_1$$ only, using fresh $$x^1 = 1$$):**
   $$z^1 = \arg\min_z \; |z| + 0(1 - z) + \frac{1}{2}(1 - z)^2 = S_1(1) = 0!$$
3. **Dual price update:**
   $$u^1 = u^0 + 1(x^1 - z^1) = 0 + (1 - 0) = 1.$$
4. **Why this is magical:**
   Notice we never had to minimize $$\frac{1}{2}x^2 + |x|$$ jointly; Step 1 was a pure linear equation and Step 2 was a pure soft-threshold! In Iteration 2, $$x^2 = \arg\min \frac{1}{2}x^2 + 1(x - 0) + \frac{1}{2}x^2 \implies 2x + 1 = 0 \implies x^2 = -0.5$$, $$z^2 = S_1(-0.5 + 1) = S_1(0.5) = 0$$, and $$u^2 = 0.5$$—closing rapidly on $$(0, 0)$$!
