Chapter 06 proved a linear rate for gradient descent under **strong convexity**: $$F(w_k)-F^\star\le c^k(F(w_0)-F^\star)$$ with $$c=1-\Theta(m/L)$$. Deep-learning losses are not strongly convex — they are not even convex — yet practitioners still see training curves that look linear on a log plot once the net is wide enough to interpolate. The **Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL)** inequality is the cheapest assumption that lets you *reuse the Chapter 06 proof* on some of those nonconvex empirical risks. This optional lesson is the bridge; it does not replace 06-03-04, and it does not re-do the saddle taxonomy of 28-01.

The note to keep on the desk is Karimi, Nutini, and Schmidt, *Linear Convergence of Gradient and Proximal-Gradient Methods under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz Condition*, [arXiv:1608.04636](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04636) (a short version appeared at ECML 2016).

## The inequality, and what it does *not* say

$$F$$ is **PL** with parameter $$\mu>0$$ on a set $$\mathcal{W}$$ if, for every $$w\in\mathcal{W}$$,

$$
\tfrac12\|\nabla F(w)\|_2^2\ge\mu\bigl(F(w)-F^\star\bigr),
$$

where $$F^\star=\inf F$$ (assumed attained). Gradient vanishing forces the optimality gap to vanish: every critical point in $$\mathcal{W}$$ is a *global* minimizer. That is already a landscape statement — no spurious local minima inside the set — without convexity.

PL does **not** give a unique minimizer. The set $$\{w:F(w)=F^\star\}$$ can be a huge affine space; overparameterized least squares is the textbook example. It does **not** imply convexity: $$F(w)=w^2+3\sin^2 w$$ is a standard one-dimensional PL function that is not convex. It does **not** certify saddles outside $$\mathcal{W}$$; you still need 28-01–28-02 for the high-dimensional saddle story.

**Strong convexity implies PL.** If $$F$$ is $$\mu$$-strongly convex and differentiable, the standard three-point identity yields $$\|\nabla F(w)\|_2^2\ge 2\mu(F(w)-F^\star)$$, which is PL with the same $$\mu$$. The converse is false, and that is the whole point of the lesson: the *rate* you proved in [06-03-04]({% multilang_post_url contents/chapter06/21-03-20-06_03_04_convergence_analysis_under_strong_convexity %}) needed a quadratic lower bound on $$F$$, but the *algebra of the descent lemma* only used PL.

## Gradient descent under PL (the Karimi reuse)

Assume $$\nabla F$$ is $$L$$-Lipschitz and $$F$$ is PL-$$\mu$$. The descent lemma with step $$\eta\in(0,1/L]$$ is the same sentence as in 06-03-01,

$$
F(w-\eta\nabla F(w))\le F(w)-\eta\bigl(1-\tfrac{L\eta}{2}\bigr)\|\nabla F(w)\|_2^2.
$$

Plug in $$\eta=1/L$$ and the PL inequality:

$$
F(w_{k+1})-F^\star
\le F(w_k)-F^\star-\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla F(w_k)\|_2^2
\le\Bigl(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\Bigr)\bigl(F(w_k)-F^\star\bigr).
$$

That is linear convergence $$F(w_k)-F^\star\le(1-\mu/L)^k(F(w_0)-F^\star)$$, with the same condition-number flavour as Chapter 06, **and we never used convexity**. Proximal gradient, coordinate descent, and a projected step have the same reuse in Karimi et al.; the pattern is always “descent lemma + PL ⇒ contract the gap.”

SGD needs an extra hypothesis: the noise must *die at minimizers*, or you must average. If $$\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f_I(w)\|_2^2]\le C\|\nabla F(w)\|_2^2$$ (a strong but standard “interpolation / overparameterized” bound), then a small constant step still contracts in expectation. If the noise floor stays positive, you recover the familiar $$O(1/\sqrt{T})$$ ball of [26-01]({% multilang_post_url contents/chapter26/2026-09-15-26_01_sgd_and_minibatch %}). Variance reduction ([26-05]({% multilang_post_url contents/chapter26/2026-09-15-26_05_variance_reduction %})) restores a linear rate on *convex* finite sums by killing that floor; PL is what lets a *nonconvex* finite sum hope for the same, locally.

## Where PL actually appears in data science

**Overparameterized least squares.** $$F(w)=\frac{1}{2n}\|Xw-y\|_2^2$$ with $$X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$$, $$d\ge n$$, and $$X$$ full row rank. Every interpolator is a global minimizer; the Hessian $$X^\top X/n$$ is only positive *semidefinite*. On the affine space of interpolators PL holds with $$\mu$$ equal to the smallest *nonzero* eigenvalue of that Hessian (restricted to the row space of $$X$$). Gradient descent from any start converges linearly to *some* interpolator — the one selected by implicit bias (28-03) — and the Chapter 06 constant $$c=1-\mu/L$$ is honest on that subspace.

**Wide nets, locally.** In several random-feature and neural-tangent regimes the training loss is *locally* PL in a neighbourhood of the initialization or of an interpolating manifold. That is the precise content of the slogan “overparameterization makes the landscape benign”: not “the loss is convex,” but “once you are close enough, PL holds and every nearby critical point interpolates.” Lesson 28-03 pointed at this inequality without proving the rate; now you have the rate.

**What PL is not.** A poorly conditioned, underparameterized, nonconvex ERM need not be PL. A ReLU net with dead units can have regions where $$\nabla F=0$$ while $$F>F^\star$$. Do not write “the network is PL” on an exam unless you name the set $$\mathcal{W}$$ and the paper that justifies it.

<div class="content-box insight-box">
<strong>The modern rate story, in one ladder.</strong>
Smooth convex GD: $$O(1/k)$$.
Strongly convex GD: linear, $$1-\Theta(m/L)$$ (06-03-04).
Nesterov / FISTA on smooth convex: $$O(1/k^2)$$ (09-05, 26-02).
SGD, convex Lipschitz: $$O(1/\sqrt{T})$$ (26-01).
SVRG / SAGA, strongly convex finite-sum: linear in incremental gradients (26-05).
PL, possibly nonconvex: the same linear GD rate as strong convexity, without uniqueness (this page).
Nonconvex DL without PL: saddles and implicit bias (28-01–28-03), not a rate you should quote.

## A two-line check

```python
import numpy as np

def pl_gap(grad_sq, f, fstar, mu):
    """Return True if the PL inequality holds at this point."""
    return 0.5 * grad_sq >= mu * (f - fstar)

# Overparameterized least squares: F(w) = 0.5/n ||Xw - y||^2
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
n, d = 20, 50
X = rng.normal(size=(n, d))
w_star = rng.normal(size=d)
y = X @ w_star                      # interpolable
# mu = smallest nonzero eigenvalue of X^T X / n, on the row space
s = np.linalg.svd(X, compute_uv=False)
mu = (s[-1] ** 2) / n
L = (s[0] ** 2) / n
print("PL parameters on range(X^T):", mu, L, "kappa", L / mu)
```

Pick a random $$w$$, compute $$F(w)$$ and $$\|\nabla F(w)\|_2^2= \|X^\top(Xw-y)/n\|_2^2$$, and check the predicate. Then run GD with $$\eta=1/L$$ and watch $$F(w_k)$$ contract by about $$1-\mu/L$$ per step — the 06-03-04 picture, now on a non-strongly-convex loss.

**Exercise 1.** Prove that $$\mu$$-strong convexity implies PL-$$\mu$$. Give a one-dimensional $$F$$ that is PL but not convex (you may use $$w^2+3\sin^2 w$$ after checking the numbers).

**Exercise 2.** On the $$n=20$$, $$d=50$$ least-squares problem above, run GD from two random starts. Confirm both reach machine-zero train loss, that the two limits differ, and that each path’s gap plot is linear on a semilog axis. How is that compatible with “no unique minimizer”?
