Ralph Gomory’s 1958 discovery is one of the most elegant algebraic tricks in all of mathematics. You do not need to stare at a graph or guess problem-specific geometry to find a cutting plane. Take *any* row of the optimal simplex tableau where a basic integer variable equals a fractional number—split every coefficient into its integer part and fractional part—and a single one-line rounding argument immediately hands you a valid inequality that strictly cuts off the fractional LP optimum.

## Background Knowledge Map: Algebraic Derivation of Gomory Fractional Cuts

- **The Fundamental Rounding Principle:** For any integer expression $$a \in \mathbb{Z}$$, $$a \le b \implies a \le \lfloor b \rfloor$$. Equivalently, if an integer equals a real expression, their fractional parts must reconcile modulo 1.
- **Reading a Fractional Simplex Row:** At the LP optimum, a basic variable $$x_i$$ satisfies $$x_i + \sum_{j \in N} \bar{a}_{ij} x_j = \bar{b}_i$$ where $$\bar{b}_i \notin \mathbb{Z}$$ and all nonbasic variables $$x_j$$ ($$j \in N$$) are currently $$0$$ and nonnegative.
- **Splitting Into Floor and Fractional Parts:** Writing $$\bar{a}_{ij} = \lfloor \bar{a}_{ij} \rfloor + f_{ij}$$ and $$\bar{b}_i = \lfloor \bar{b}_i \rfloor + f_i$$ with $$f_{ij} \in [0, 1)$$ and $$f_i \in (0, 1)$$, moving integer terms to the left shows $$\sum_{j \in N} f_{ij} x_j - f_i \in \mathbb{Z}$$. Because $$\sum_{j \in N} f_{ij} x_j \ge 0$$ and $$f_i < 1$$, this integer must be $$\ge 0$$!
- **Automatic Strict Separation:** The resulting **Gomory fractional cut** $$\sum_{j \in N} f_{ij} x_j \ge f_i$$ is *always* violated by the current LP optimum (since $$x_j = 0$$ for $$j \in N$$ gives $$0 < f_i$$) and is *always* valid for every integer feasible solution.

### Step-by-Step Worked Example (Read Slowly): Deriving a Gomory Cut From a Simplex Tableau Row

Suppose at the LP optimum, every variable (including slacks $$s_1, s_2$$) must be a nonnegative integer, and one row of the optimal simplex tableau reads:
$$x_1 + \frac{3}{2} s_1 - \frac{1}{4} s_2 = \frac{7}{4},$$
where $$x_1 = \frac{7}{4}$$ is basic and $$s_1 = s_2 = 0$$ are nonbasic.

1. **Compute the floor and fractional part of every coefficient** (remember: $$\lfloor -1/4 \rfloor = -1$$, NOT $$0$$!):
   - $$\bar{b}_1 = \frac{7}{4} = 1 + \frac{3}{4} \implies f_1 = \frac{3}{4}$$.
   - $$\bar{a}_{1, s_1} = \frac{3}{2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2} \implies f_{1, s_1} = \frac{1}{2}$$.
   - $$\bar{a}_{1, s_2} = -\frac{1}{4} = (-1) + \frac{3}{4} \implies f_{1, s_2} = \frac{3}{4}$$.
2. **Rewrite the row separating integers from fractional parts:**
   $$\underbrace{\bigl(x_1 + 1 \cdot s_1 - 1 \cdot s_2 - 1\bigr)}_{\in\ \mathbb{Z}} = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{2} s_1 - \frac{3}{4} s_2.$$
3. **Apply the Gomory inequality:** Since the LHS is an integer and $$\frac{1}{2} s_1 + \frac{3}{4} s_2 \ge 0$$, the RHS is at most $$\frac{3}{4}$$, so it must be $$\le 0$$:
   $$\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{2} s_1 - \frac{3}{4} s_2 \le 0 \iff \frac{1}{2} s_1 + \frac{3}{4} s_2 \ge \frac{3}{4} \iff 2 s_1 + 3 s_2 \ge 3.$$
4. **Verify strict separation:** At the current LP optimum $$(s_1, s_2) = (0, 0)$$, the cut evaluates to $$0 \ge 3$$—strictly violated! Yet any integer solution satisfying the original equation satisfies $$2 s_1 + 3 s_2 \ge 3$$.

Mathematician Gomory devised a method to easily find valid inequalities based on the following fact:
>
if $$a \le b$$ and $$a$$ is an integer then $$a \le \lfloor b \rfloor$$.

That is, if a is an integer, then even if b is rounded down, the relationship that a is less than or equal to b is maintained.

## Gomory fractional cut
Let's say that the feasible set $$S$$ defined by the convex hull of the IP problem mentioned earlier is as follows:

$$ S \subseteq \left\{ x \in \mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+} : \sum^{n}_{j=1} a_{j} x_{j} = a_{0} \right\} \quad \text{where} \quad  a_{0} \notin \mathbb{Z}$$

In this case, the Gomory fractional cut is defined as follows:

$$ \sum^{n}_{j=1} (a_{j} - \lfloor a_{j} \rfloor) x_{j} \ge a_{0} -  \lfloor a_{0} \rfloor $$

There are many ideas that extend this concept. For example, there are Chvatal cuts, split cuts, lift-and-project cuts, etc.

The derivation process of Gomory fractional cut is detailed on Wikipedia, so please refer to it.

* For detailed information, see [Cutting-plane method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting-plane_method)
