In 2009, Amir Beck and Marc Teboulle published a paper that became one of the most cited optimization articles of the twenty-first century: **FISTA** (*Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm*). Built directly on Nesterov's acceleration, FISTA replaces the simple linear weight $$\frac{k-2}{k+1}$$ with the exact golden-ratio recurrence $$t_{k+1} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4t_k^2}}{2}$$, turning ISTA into a blazing-fast workhorse for image deblurring, compressed sensing, and large-scale Lasso.

## Background Knowledge Map: Anatomy of the FISTA Recurrence

- **Pillar 1 — The Quadratic Recurrence for $$t_k$$:** Starting from $$t_0 = 1$$, the update $$t_{k+1} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4t_k^2}}{2}$$ is the exact positive root of $$t_{k+1}^2 - t_{k+1} = t_k^2$$. That algebraic identity is what makes the Lyapunov proof telescope cleanly and guarantees $$t_k \ge \frac{k+1}{2}$$.
- **Pillar 2 — Momentum Weight $$\frac{t_{k-1} - 1}{t_k}$$:** Using $$\beta_k = \frac{t_{k-1} - 1}{t_k}$$ gives $$\beta_1 = 0$$ on the first step and approaches $$1 - \frac{3}{k}$$ as $$k$$ grows, closely matching Nesterov's $$3/k$$ friction law while tightening the constant in the $$O(1/k^2)$$ bound.
- **Pillar 3 — FISTA for Lasso:** Setting $$g(x) = \frac{1}{2}\|Ax - b\|_2^2$$ and $$h(x) = \lambda\|x\|_1$$ makes the FISTA step $$x^{(k)} = S_{\lambda / L}(y^{(k)} - \frac{1}{L} A^\top (A y^{(k)} - b))$$, where $$L = \|A\|_2^2 = \lambda_{\max}(A^\top A)$$.
- **Pillar 4 — Practical Extensions (MFISTA & Restarting):** Because FISTA can exhibit non-monotonic ripples, Beck & Teboulle introduced Monotone FISTA (MFISTA), which compares $$f(x^{(k)})$$ against $$f(x^{(k-1)})$$ and keeps the lower-objective point while preserving $$O(1/k^2)$$ convergence.

### Step-by-Step Worked Example (Read Slowly): Computing the Exact FISTA Sequence $$t_k$$ and Momentum Weights $$\beta_k$$

Let's compute the first four scalar weights $$t_k$$ and momentum factors $$\beta_k = \frac{t_{k-1} - 1}{t_k}$$ by hand, starting from $$t_0 = 1$$.

1. **Step $$k = 1$$:**
   - $$t_1 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(1)^2}}{2} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180$$ (literally the golden ratio $$\phi$$!).
   - Momentum weight: $$\beta_1 = \frac{t_0 - 1}{t_1} = \frac{1 - 1}{1.6180} = 0$$.
   - Check identity: $$t_1^2 - t_1 = \phi^2 - \phi = 1 = t_0^2$$.
2. **Step $$k = 2$$:**
   - $$t_2 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(1.6180)^2}}{2} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 10.4721}}{2} = \frac{1 + 3.3871}{2} \approx 2.1935$$.
   - Momentum weight: $$\beta_2 = \frac{t_1 - 1}{t_2} = \frac{0.6180}{2.1935} \approx 0.2817$$.
3. **Step $$k = 3$$:**
   - $$t_3 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4(2.1935)^2}}{2} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{20.246}}{2} \approx \frac{1 + 4.4996}{2} \approx 2.7498$$.
   - Momentum weight: $$\beta_3 = \frac{t_2 - 1}{t_3} = \frac{1.1935}{2.7498} \approx 0.4340$$.
4. **Check the growth lower bound $$t_k \ge (k+1)/2$$:**
   - $$t_1 \approx 1.618 \ge 1.0$$, $$t_2 \approx 2.194 \ge 1.5$$, $$t_3 \approx 2.750 \ge 2.0$$. Because $$f(x^{(k)}) - f^\star \le \frac{L \|x^{(0)} - x^\star\|_2^2}{2 t_{k-1}^2}$$, this steady linear growth of $$t_k$$ is the exact engine behind FISTA's $$O(1/k^2)$$ decay.

# Example: FISTA

In this section, we introduce an example of the Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA), which is an accelerated proximal gradient method.

## FISTA Algorithm
The FISTA algorithm solves problems of the form:
> $$\min_x g(x) + h(x)$$
where $$g$$ is convex and differentiable with an $$L$$-Lipschitz gradient, and $$h$$ is convex.

With a fixed step size $$\alpha \le 1/L$$ (for example $$\alpha = 1/L$$), the update steps are:
1. Initialize $$x^{(0)} = x^{(-1)}$$, $$t_0 = 1$$.
2. For $$k = 1, 2, ...$$:
   - $$t_k = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 t_{k-1}^2}}{2}$$
   - $$y^{(k)} = x^{(k-1)} + \frac{t_{k-1} - 1}{t_k} (x^{(k-1)} - x^{(k-2)})$$
   - $$x^{(k)} = \text{prox}_{\alpha}\left(y^{(k)} - \alpha \nabla g(y^{(k)})\right)$$

Here $$t_k$$ is only the momentum sequence; it is not a step size. The step size in the prox step stays fixed at $$\alpha$$. (With backtracking, $$\alpha$$ may shrink, but it is still a separate quantity from $$t_k$$.)

## Application to Lasso
FISTA can be applied to Lasso regression, which involves minimizing a least squares loss with an $$L_1$$ regularization term.

FISTA achieves faster convergence compared to standard proximal gradient methods.
